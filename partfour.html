Hazy Views and Taller Trees

Hello friends and family

I am writing to you from Amsterdam dressed in a sweater and jeans, missing the endless time I felt were embedded in my days. My final night on Mo’orea I had been sitting outside writing my final blog post when two young men from UPF came over to say hello. They had stopped by Gump to see if anyone was around to chat with. I had a lovely time talking to them and reflecting on my time there and making new friends even on my last night. What I had been writing on my computer before they walked up was,

I have spent the entire day feeling completely grateful. I feel so calm and content, and although this feeling won’t last for long I feel so lucky to feel it at all. I realize that not only is this the end of my program and time in Mo’orea, but also the end of my Junior year. Although I am sad about this program ending, I feel satisfied knowing the ending, now I can know this experience went well and was everything I imagined and more. This feeling carries for what was for the entirety of my Junior year, too. It was my favorite so far, I don’t think there is any one particular reason why, but I feel in fuller bloom as a human than ever before.

I had been in Chicago for the weekend before I flew to Amsterdam, and being in a city again felt exciting and vibrant, and satisfying that my body and mind knew to switch into city mode, and made sure I was on time to catch the bus and transfer to the train. The twenty or so minute the bus ride from Schipol to the apartment meant that I was still for long enough to realize how much my eyes have witnessed within the last month – the jarring mountains no longer surround me, but I am instead met with hazy views with tall trees complicating my cloudgazing desires. As for the adjustment and what I will take with me, sometimes it does feel a bit strange to be around so many people at once. I may have remembered how to adjust to the train schedules, but I have not adjusted to ignoring people I pass. I find myself accidentally smiling at the Dutch neighbors I pass, humbled once I remember through their very stern gaze back that I am in a very different cultural landscape.

Although it is hard to adapt life from Mo'orea to my life here or in the States, I am really happy knowing something as simple as that I enjoy being a morning person. Or, that I do need to sit and gaze out into the distance. Simple and tangible lessons like that feel really special to be able to bring with me from my experience. I also think that the wide variety and depth of classes will stay with me much longer than I realize, and I wonder how it will influence the next few years, not only in the way I might travel but also how I approach my career. I also know more about a place I had barely thought of, and that has changed me too. I know that being friendly to strangers is important to me, and the sense of community I grew up with is going to be important to me for years to come.

More soon. We will have to see.

Rebekah
