Reflection of Night Snorkeling and Brief Ramblings

Hello friends and family…

here are some extra thoughts I have that I wanted to write down, not the weekly update but rather more of a ramble, so feel free to skip.

Last night we went night snorkeling, which was unlike anything I had ever done. It was incredibly disorienting, and humbling to see how limited my vision became. I was snorkeling with a girl named Laura, and we made the mistake of swimming against the current instead of going with it. A mistake we wouldn’t have made if it were in the daylight, but this is a good example of making stupid decisions when you’re nervous. We soon agreed to follow the current, but were already into the thick coral, and the current was strong enough that it was pushing us along. I was grateful I wore shorts over my bathing suit or else I would have had a pretty gnarly scratch on my butt. As we were swimming out, I would momentarily become scared that I had lost Laura because when I would turn around my fins blocked her flashlight shining through, and all I saw was darkness. Luckily she was right behind me. The clouds were covering the moon so it was very dark out, and I would have not wanted to be out alone.

We made it to a calmer section of the lagoon and it became much more of a pleasurable experience. The coral was dull and the life roaming around felt like a different ecosystem– sea cucumbers (rori) that we see flat on the ground during the day, were in an upward facing dog pose releasing their version of eggs. We saw urchins out, an octopus, and a blue sting ray. Laura and I had seen rays sleeping during the day, but at night it looked like it was flying. It was so beautiful, illuminated by our flashlights. At the time I thought it reminded me of a bat, even though sitting here now, it does not resemble them in the slightest. It was large, fluid and beautiful moving through the shallow waters and eventually out of sight. Sometimes when we find more “rare” animals like sharks or rays, people sort of run towards them, and thankfully as the days go on I am no longer initially scared by sharks or rays, but to be honest with you I had very little interest in chasing this ray into the dark of the night, a night that it knows much better than I. I was grateful that we saw it, but the creatures out at night did not want to see the shine of our flashlight for a reason, so it felt like we were rather imposing. As we continued our swim and I was calm, I appreciated how familiar the reef felt. Maybe I am not as situated as I think I am, but when we were snorkeling I recognized large clusters of coral and was able to know just about where I was in relation to the shore, which was quite important considering the shore was very dimly lit.

Today we went out into the lagoon for hour-long observations of a cubic meter, and the journey was more pleasant. Emerson described the current as a lazy lagoon, and it was much easier to trust the current when I could see my surroundings. Again, lightness comes to mind. Not only from Calvino but also just in everything, from the conversations we have in class about the importance of color for ecosystems and diversity, to the protection from the sun. Even though it is only Wednesday I am already aware that our time here is so limited, and soon we will be moving to Gump Station. It feels odd to be somewhere that represents the epitome of paradise, and feel like I am not soaking it up enough. I woke up this morning and saw out the window someone doing yoga, and I thought to myself that I ought to do that too, or else I’d regret not being able to look back on that. Kind of an odd thing to dwell on given everything else we are doing, especially the fact that I’ve never done that sort of thing. Hopefully I will become that person, but not everything has to happen all at once.

Non-snorkeling thoughts…

Something else I have been thinking about is a dynamic between the class and the lecturer. Most of this takes place in an informal setting in our living room. Much of the time we are listening to different experts doing a slideshow presentation or just talking off the top of their head, answering any question coming their way. Yesterday, we were showing photos of our time in the water and either identifying the species or asking our Professor for help to identify. When he would get it wrong, someone would look it up on Google or Google images and begin to argue with him. Now, pushing back is a part of learning and not my concern here, but rather, unanimously people were willing to trust Chat GPT or Google Gemini over someone who has probably written the paper that Chat is pulling from, or knows the person who did. The Google Search Image was a really provocative example because the Professor didn’t know the answer to the species, and then when Google was able to identify it, he quickly said “oh duh I should have known that”, and then it seemed as though the collective trust in his knowledge declined ever so slightly. This feels like a real predicament for educators, and especially someone like him, since he has dedicated his whole life to knowing taxonomy, and working on the sequencing data to better understand the intricacies of the environment. Should he instead be more structured, and teach us how to identify instead of telling us? I mean this in saying, is it not so much passing along the literal knowledge that is important, but teaching the construction of the knowledge? I don’t know. It also reminded me of the time when I doubted myself because I had googled something that was contradictory to what I had read in multiple books. Gemini was dubious at best, but for a few moments I was ready to concede before I realized how ridiculous it was that I didn’t trust my own knowledge and the context I knew that supported it. I imagine the dynamic of search engines, no matter the form, was much more provocative decades before this when computers and the internet arrived in the classroom, but it feels like yet another symptom of the way we exchange information and how it is changing.

This is also accompanied with the way that when getting ready for bed, many people will be scrolling on Instagram reels while brushing their teeth, or in small periods of waiting lulls. I don’t say this in a judgmental way, I have done this too. It is just more poignant to me now that I have been away from it long enough to see how strange we are becoming? Our minds don’t have time to rest, and be bored, which the more that I am bored the more I think it is good for me. It’s also interesting that some people will spend hours in the back of the house away from any scenery on their phones, instead of sitting out and appreciating the scenery. Again, I don’t even say this with judgment, I just want to note it, and wonder if we had been conditioned to appreciate something such as scenery, and now it is not as special since it has been captured, and shareable across unimaginable lengths, as a photo. But also there is the same number of people who spend time outside playing the guitar, reading, swimming, exercising, or playing board games. I suppose it is all relative.
