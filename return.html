Return to the Hammock

Hello friends and family,

This week has been unlike any other week here: I have been mostly inside. My commute is across the street, where I often walk barefoot across the hot pavement because I have misplaced my flip flops. We started our new class, Oceania Culture and Society with Alexander Mawyer , who is engaging and smart– I have been loving his class. He is very silly, but I think in part his teaching strategy is to be goofy as a way to make himself less intimidating, which has been effective: our class is very talkative, a sign of a good teacher. I have been able to speak to him after class and luckily he is well versed in the post-structuralists and the worlds of thinkers I have begun to slowly work my way through. He suggests I read Isaiah Berlin, which I am eager to dive into. Through our discussions, which he has been so generous to spend the time with me to do, I realize that the bridge between my interest in Jewish question and post-structuralism is because so many of the thinkers are inevitably Jewish, and also post-structralism is so wack and bonkers. It reminds me of something Elizabeth (a common mention, she has so many good ideas!) spoke about when she was taking her Freud class, where Freud discusses a Jewish soul. I have thought of that ever since, because many of my favorite people are not Jews, but I often do feel a particular connection to a fellow Jew. I wonder how much of the feeling is the little girl in me, when meeting a Jew felt so rare and far between, or if Freud was on to something. Time will tell. Back to Mawyer. It has been so exciting to be using the analytical side of my brain again and to have someone in person to exchange some of these ideas rattling in my head. The other day I went to the store to buy a small pocket notebook. I have one at school I carry at all times, for obscure ideas or for a last minute lecture I sneak into, but I left it at home. Standing in my room with my bag on my back, right as we were heading out the door I looked at this small notebook, and decided to leave it behind. A mistake, but a good lesson to learn: it is important to keep those small companions with me. I was naive in believing my ideas would halt while I was here. But I also feel relaxed and lethargic unlike I can remember (this may be in part to the coffee here being extraordinarily week). If I feel this way, I am reminded of COVID, and I feel uneasy: feeling like I am wasting my days away. Luckily here I don’t feel as though I am wasting the days away but rather that I am resting, not to say that I am not exploring too.

One day in class, Chris Meyers (our previous Professor) walked through the door with his wife Sarah and a new character, Steve (who I thought was French given his frail frame, fedora and striped t-shirt but turned out to be Australian) and announced that Steve found a snake in the shower(!) As far as they know snakes have not been recorded on Mo’orea before, and this type of parthenogenetic snake was only recorded on Tahiti in 2014. We erupted into clapping and passed around the very small snake that I would have mistaken with a worm, in a small test tube.

As a part of our class on Tuesday we woke up early and joined a Va’a team of 14-15 year old’s who took us out into the bay. They are fundraising to go to New Zealand for a competition that they were invited to. They will be representing French Polynesia in this Oceanic competition. Our assistant program director, Jennyfer set it up, her little brother is on the team. It was a humbling arm and core workout, but so so fun. When I am traveling I always wish I could be a part of some sort of ordinary activity that locals do. Middle school aged activities is not usually what I am thinking of, but nevertheless it was so lovely to see life here a different way. It was also close to my heart becuase it is a part of the culutral revival movement here, and all across Oceania, and made me think of The Yiddish Culture Club whcih is very different in many ways, but has added another dimension in realizing how difficult cutlural revival really is. In class we speak of language extincition, and I was reminded of one club meeting where I tried saying a sentence in Yiddish and how foreign it felt on my tounge (also that it disappointingly sounded very German).

For a few afternoons Emerson’s roommate Henry and I have been helping Emerson set up a sailboat that he found. After a few days he found the sail and the ropes (or lines, if you are a sailor) and has been taking it out. It is a small one person laser but if all goes well he said he’d take me out for a sail, which I am looking forward to! About twenty seconds walk from Gump is a bar, where the mixed drinks are $20. Beer it is. But, it turns out uninteresting conversations at the dinner table are no different from uninteresting conversations at a bar. Some nights there have been such heavy rain showers they wake me up in the middle of the night, but at lunch the next day we have all forgotten about the midnight interruption becuase the intense sun has dried up any ponds that accumulated overnight. There is a grass lawn where we have volleyball games– luckily my Freshman year JV volleyball skills haven’t rusted up too poorly. Instead of gopher holes here, there are crabs retreating into their holes. It reminds me of the gophers Peter would waterlog at school. Nice to be reminded of home!

Last night I was laying out on the dock staring that the stars, which was much more pleasurable than laying out avoiding the gaze of the sun. I realized when I was stargazing I didn’t know what I was looking at – at all. I eventually pulled out SkyView on my phone, but I knew that the app was not telling me facts, but was just some traditions understanding of the stars (Greeks or whatever), but that is not what I grew up with, nevertheless, I have no way of knowing the way that the people who came before me read the stars. Although I do find it helpful to know which planets I am staring at. As I was gazing (and sitting here in retrospect, I guess I was having quite a pessimistic viewing) the dock was proving to be quite unstable ground, moving at unpredictable moments, never drastic but rather abrupt despite the bay beneath me being calm. I stayed out with the swaying and the boats hitting against the rim of the dock, reminding me why people are scared of the dark. I didn’t feel like said “people” until I could no longer bear the weird tonal shifts the bridge kept making, some high pitched and short, others low and long. I declared it good enough and I returned to my hammock.

In all honesty I feel incomplete with my thoughts and words as I upload them to you. I hope to add more soon, I suppose the words are not coming together in the way they usually do. I am sending my well wishes. Talk soon!

Rebekah
