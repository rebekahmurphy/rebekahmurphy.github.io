Stillness and Daydreaming Clouding the Days

Hello friends and family,

Once again, I have found myself unable to put this past week into the amount of words it deserves. The more busy the week, it seems, the harder it is to sit down and write. I know I will lose out on important key moments, but all is well. Just know as the reader, there are some colors in the painting that are missing.

Yesterday we moved to Gump Station. It feels like we are re-connected to others on the island, no longer secluded by a small dirt road accessed by route of another. I’ve enjoyed swaying slowly in my hammock overlooking Cook’s Bay seeing an occasional group of jet skis, families going fishing, and people rowing their canoes.

A few nights ago the man who gave us our food tour at the beginning of the program, Heimata, came over to discuss local politics given that he is running for Mayor next month (here is an article where he is quoted, if you’re interested) . He is about 45 and spoke about how when he was growing up there was still no electricity on the island, only dirt roads, and very few grocery stores. In his lifetime he said he has seen Mo’orea and the fellow Society Islands go through massive change (Bora Bora, as the most drastic example), and though he was clear to say that he is not anti-development, he emphasized the importance of managing the development. There is a plan to bring in a larger shopping mall (there are already a few here, but this would be like bringing a Costco when there is already a Safeway). The question being, do they need a Costco on Mo’orea if there is already one on Tahiti? Being in Cook’s Bay now seeing the homes beginning to be built on newly cleared land further and further up the hill, I would suspect that if I were to return here as a 45 year old woman, many new homes would be covering the fairly vacant hillside that I see today. Time will tell. As for now, I feel incredibly lucky that I can sway on the hammock reading Joan Didion’s Let Me Tell You What I Mean , a wonderful birthday gift from my dear friend Elizabeth, who I hold in close association with Didion, especially since the purse incident in Paris last summer.

Periodically I would try and write down a list of what we had done over the week on a google doc to try and keep track. The list includes: Time on a jet ski, Sea sickness and swimming with sharks and rays, Politics of French Polynesia, Tahiti Film Festival, E-DNA sampling, I enjoy not wearing shoes, although I am grateful to have them it is lovely to not need them. It seems as though I have covered politics, and as for the last on… I suppose it speaks for itself although it is a bit strange. We are all friends here. I shall begin explaining my list.

We went to Hannah’s work, Coral Gardeners , which had a smelled a bit off when Hannah showed us a promo video the first week, and after our visit I was pleased with my intuition. I felt like I had entered a real life SNL skit about a collaboration between Silicon Valley and surfers pretending they are uncovering science for the first time– and were charging $300 for you to see it. Their headquarters had a lot of LED lights against their signature black backdrop, and a touchscreen mirror in the bathroom – even though the doors opened backwards in the cramped hallway. However, I will get off my high horse to say that I was able to get on a jet ski – and it was so fun. I was smiling ear to ear. Everything about it, the speed in which we were going, the feeling of the air, the way the light hit the water outside of the lagoon and inside of it, and the abruptness of the mountains laying out before us. I had been feeling pretty sad in the morning, but in that moment of the afternoon I felt a profound sense of satisfaction: I am seeing the world at many different perspectives and speeds. I made sure to take a few memory photos (where you put your hands up like you’re taking a photo, but it’s imaginary so you look a little stupid – emphasis on squinting your eye and doing a half smile, for optimal success, of course).

A few days before the fast speed of the jet ski, we went on a small boat past the lagoon to take E-DNA samples, which is apparently all the rage now. If you take one liter of water and put it through a test tube with a filter you can detect which animals have gone through the water because of the DNA they are shedding. It is not a complete slam dunk because some animals don’t shed as much DNA as others, etc. but we went around the island to take different samples and sent it back to New Zealand with the director of the Winderloo Foundation for analysis, who came to visit us for a few days. I know this science and technological coupling is all very cool and important to someone but unfortunately I am not the gal to be earth shatteringly excited by this– I’m impressed as a human and everything but I don’t know enough to appreciate the magnitude of it all. However, when we were collecting the sampling we were in semi rough waters and I learned the hard way that I do indeed get sea sick. Luckily once we were back in the lagoon the water felt forgiving again, and we anchored at a tourist attraction where you can swim with tons and tons of reef sharks and rays in shallow white sand waters.

We had two birthdays in our group this week, and it included lots of flower crown making and dancing after dinner. It was very sweet. My new friend Laura and I were having an avid discussion Marx, and differences in perceptions of communism in American compared to China. So we missed out on the first Abba song of the night, but were able to jump in for the rest.

We went to Tahiti on Thursday and saw the opening day of the Film Festival. The first one was in French and it was very interesting to try and understand what was going on, since it was a documentary and those are mostly dialogue with dramatic shots. For example, one of the men (that I understood at the time to have lost his son) was painting his front door green. I didn’t know if this was an artistic analogy I was supposed to be reading into, a man painting his home? What does green represent? New beginnings? Fresh start? I suspect it was just an ordinary shot with important dialogue in the back I did not understand. But all of the b-roll (which repeated itself, I have to say) was shot at night and in places where you would have seen the beautiful tropical water, it was dark and barren. The water during the day was focused on rivers through different neighborhoods. It was really interesting to see Tahiti through local eyes, it looked much more what I imagine certain parts of Southeast Asia look like rather than the turquoise water I am exposed to every day given that I am a tourist. Thanks to Sophie, the one person in the program who speaks French, she told us that it was about the meth crisis in the Society Islands, and that most of the meth could be found in Middle Schools where children as young as 12-13 are dealing the meth. Crazy. So sad. The dealers apparently tell them that they can charge it to their parents to get them hooked, and then they will rob their parents. Heartbreaking. We also saw a movie about PFAS which is in everything and it seems like the story of being this generation (although I hate that word): knowing a corporation in the 80s decided to keep making a product they knew would be deadly for the world, and now there is simply nothing to do about it! Great. There were a lot of stills of people and their brain surgeries and discussions of all the various cancers you could get from the forever chemical. Very upsetting. All to say, the remainder of Thursday and the following day I felt a bit destroyed and defeated. A familiar feeling, but rather than at school or at home, I have the time and capacity to sit with the feelings in a deeper way. And here, the bonkers ability to be able to go for a solo snorkel also made me feel much better.

Now that I have access to reliable internet again I am enjoying perusing the web, and falling into rabbit holes like looking more into Michael Pollan, but more importantly, his much cooler artist wife, Judith Belzer. I hope to dive back into the Jewish questions that engrossed me so greatly last semester, and the larger world implications that seemed to expose these questions to be concerning humanity and not only Jews. But I also don’t want to break the cloud of stillness that has been fogging me these past weeks, allowing me to read most of my books and do much needed daydreaming. It is interesting how some people on the program cannot stand stillness, needing to be doing something with their time. Others seem more content, and others are perhaps sitting physically still, but are digitally active in their scrolling of Reels. Who knows. None of my business. I’m lightly observing rather than judging. I promise.

As always, I am wishing you the best, and I hope that you can find moments in your day with clouds or of stillness. I think of you often and look forward to the next time we speak.

Until next time!

Rebekah
